**Preliminary definition:** A short exact sequence in $\Pr^{\dual}$ is a sequence $C \to D \to E$ in $\Pr^{\dual}$ which is both a fiber and cofiber sequence in $\PrL_{\st}$.

**Remark:** We will see that short exact sequences in $\Pr^{\dual}$ are also fiber and cofiber sequences in $\Pr^{\dual}$.

**Proposition:** The functor $\Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}\colon \Pr^{\dual} \to \Cat^{\perf}$ preserves short exact sequences.
**Proof sketch:** Let $C \hookrightarrow D \twoheadrightarrow E$ be a short exact sequence in $\Pr^{\dual}$. Then we get a Verdier sequence$$C^{\kappa} \hookrightarrow D^{\kappa} \twoheadrightarrow E^{\kappa}$$(argument omitted). Hence also $$\Ind(C^{\kappa}) \to \Ind(D^{\kappa}) \twoheadrightarrow \Ind(E^{\kappa})$$is a short exact sequence. Since colimits commute with colimits, this shows that the sequence$$\Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}(C) \to \Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}(D) \to \Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}(E)$$is a cofiber sequence.

It now remains to show that $\Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}(-)$ preserves fully faithful functors. We can detect fully faithful functors on $\Ind(-)$. Consider the following diagram:$$\begin{CD}C @>\hat{y}>> \Ind(C^{\kappa}) @>>> \Ind(\Calk^{\cont}_{\kappa}(C)) \\

**Definition:** We denote by $\Pr^{LL}_{\st} \subseteq \PrL_{\st}$ the wide subcategory containing the strongly continuous functors.

**Remark:** We have full subcategory inclusions$$\PrL_{\st,\omega} \subseteq \Pr^{\dual} \subseteq \Pr^{LL}_{\st}.$$
**Proposition:** Let $D$ be a stable presentable category and let F_i\colon C_i \to D$ be strongly continuous functors for $i \in I$. Assume that each $C_i$ is dualizable. Suppose that $D$ is generated under colimits by the essential images of the functors $F_i$. Then also $D$ is dualizable.
**Proof:** We first reduce to the case $I = \{*\}$. The essential images of the functors $F_i$ generate the same subcategory under colimits as the induced functor$$\prod_i C_i \to D.$$We claim that the product $\prod_i C_i$ is dualizable. To see this, recall that each $C_i$ is a retract of some compactly generated category $\widetilde{C}_i$. Then also the product $\prod_i \widetilde{C}_i$ is compactly generated, and it admits $\prod_i C_i$ as a retract. It follows that $\prod_i C_i$ is dualizable, as claimed.

We've thus reduced to the case of a single strongly continuous functor $F\colon C \to D$ from a dualizable category $C$ whose image generates $D$ under colimits. We now show that $D$ is dualizable. Recall that it suffices to show that arbitrary products distribute over filtered colimits. The right adjoint $F^R\colon D \to C$ is conservative (as the image of $F$ generates $D$) and it preserves both limits and colimits. So we may check that arbitrary products distribute over filtered colimits by checking it in $C$. But in $C$ it holds because $C$ is dualizable.

**Proposition:** The subcategories$$\Pr^{\dual} \subseteq \Pr^{LL}_{\st} \subseteq \PrL_{\st}$$are closed under colimits and are cocomplete.
**Proof:** We first show the claim for the second inclusion. Let $I \to \Pr^{LL}_{\st} \subseteq \PrL_{\st}$ be a diagram. Its colimit in $\PrL_{\st}$ may be identified with the diagram of right adjoints in $\PrR_{\st}$. By assumption, this diagram consists of colimit-preserving functors, so this diagram of right adjoints also lives in $\PrL_{\st}$. Since the forgetful functor $\PrL_{\st} \to \Cat$ preserves limits, it follows that the resulting limit cone $\lim_i C_i \to C_i$ consists of colimit-preserving functors. This implies that the original colimit cocone in $\PrL_{\st}$ consists of strongly continuous functors, i.e.\ forms a diagram in $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$. Similarly, a cocone of strongly continuous functors induces a strongly continuous functor out of the colimit. This shows that the second inclusion is closed under colimits.

Now we prove the claim for the first inclusion. For a diagram $I \to \Pr^{\dual}$, the functors $C_i \to \colim^{\PrL_{\st}} C_i$ jointly generate the colimit, since the functor $\lim_i \to \prod_i C_i$ is conservative. Sine $\prod_i C_i$ is dualizable, it follows from Proposition 2.53 that also the colimit in $\PrL_{\st}$ is dualizable.

**Lemma:** Let $I \to \PrL_{\st}$ be a filtered diagram. Let $C := \colim_i^{\PrL_{\st}} C_i$, let $F_i\colon C_i \to C$ be the coprojections, and let $F_{i,k}\colon C_i \to C_k$ be the transition functors. Their right adjoints are denoted with $(-)^R$. Then we have:
(a) There is an equivalence $\colim_i F_i F_i^R \simeq \id_C$.
(b) Suppose the diagram factors through $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$. Then we have:$$\colim_{k \geq i,j} F_{j,k}^R F_{i,k} \simeq F_j^R F_i.$$**Proof:** We use that $C \simeq \lim_i C_i$ along the transition functors $F_{i,j}^R$ and projections $F_i^R\colon C \to C_i$. Then we have
(a) $$\Hom_C(-,-) \simeq \lim_i \Hom_{C_i}(F_i^R(-),F_i^R(-)) \simeq \lim_i \Hom_C(F_iF_i^R(-),-) \simeq \Hom(\colim_i F_i F_i^R(-), (-)).$$
For (b) we only give a sketch, as it is more technical. Since $F_{j,k}^R$ preserves colimits, we define$$F_{i,j}'(x) := (\colim_{k \geq i,j} F^R_{j,k} F_{i,k}(x))$$The right-hand side is compatible with the transition functors, so this induces a functor $F_i'\colon C_i \to C$. Then claim (b) is equivalent to the claim that $F_i \simeq F_i'$.  But we have $F_j^R(F_i') \simeq \colim_{k \geq i,j} F_{j,k}^R F_{i,k} \overset{!}{\simeq} F_j^R F_i.$ One can show this by using a similar verification: $F_i' \dashv F_i^R$, $F_i \vdash F_i^R$, so $F_i \simeq F_i'$.

**Proposition:** The following statements hold:
(a) The class of fully faithful functors in $\Pr^{\dual}$ or $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$ is closed under filtered colimits.
(b) Filtered colimits in $\Pr^{\dual}$ or $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$ preserve short exact sequences.
**Proof:** We show the claim for $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$. Let $I$ be a filtered category and let $(C_i)$ and $(D_i)$ be $I$-indexed diagrams in $\Pr^{LL}_{\st}$. We can reduce to showing that for all $i,j$ the map$$F_j^R F_i \to F_j^R \Phi^R \Phi F_i$$is a natural isomorphism: the $F_j^R$ are jointly conservative and the images of the $F_i$ jointly generate $C$ under colimits.
