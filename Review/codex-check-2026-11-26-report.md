### A6. Mathew’s componentwise compact rational generators (OK)

Chirantan Chowdhury’s notes of Mathew’s lectures state the description in Remark 6.18, p. 14. Read the transition condition for $r<s$, as in Ramzi’s Definition 4.20. Ramzi’s Definitions 4.20 and 4.22 and Corollary 4.37 give the rational-generator construction in the Ind-category of the ordinary limit using the ideal of componentwise compact maps. The following argument gives the sharper bound on the **terms** and places the generators in Chapter 7’s precise ambient category. [Mathew’s Bonn lectures, September 2024](https://www.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/de/webfm_send/881/1), [Ramzi, v1, Sections 4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21537v1)

Write $L=\lim_i^{\mathrm{dual}} C_i$, $A=\lim_i C_i^{\omega_1}$ and $j:L\hookrightarrow M=\operatorname{Ind}(A)$. For an eligible diagram $F:\mathbb Q\to A$, its proposed generator is $X=\operatorname{colim}_r y_A F(r)$ in $M$. Put $X_t=\operatorname{colim}_{r<t}y_A F(r)$. In each coordinate, a compact transition lifts through $\hat y$ of its target, so its image in the Calkin quotient is null. A cofinal sequence approaching $t$ now has zero telescope in that quotient. Hence every $X_t$ belongs to the componentwise kernel $K$ of Chapter 7. For $t<u$, the transition $X_t\to X_u$ factors through the compact object $y_A F(t)$ of $M$. The manuscript’s **cor:Dualizable_Limit_Generators** therefore puts $\operatorname{colim}_t X_t=X$ in $j(L)$.

Conversely, for $z\in L^{\omega_1}$ choose a rational exhaustion $z_r$ *in* $L^{\omega_1}$ with compact transitions by **prop:Compact_Interpolation**(3). The projections $p_i:L\to C_i$ preserve both $\omega_1$-compact objects and compact morphisms by strong continuity. They define $P:L^{\omega_1}\to A$. In Step 1’s adjunction the embedding $j$ corresponds to this very cone, so $j\simeq\operatorname{Ind}(P)\hat y_L$. Compactness of the transitions gives $\hat y_L(z)\simeq\operatorname{colim}_r y_L(z_r)$ by restricting to a cofinal sequence and using the lifting characterization of compact maps. Thus $j(z)\simeq\operatorname{colim}_r y_A P(z_r)$. Such $z$ generate $L$. The supplied rational diagrams already contain the intermediate stages needed in the first direction. This proof requires no coherent factorization of an arbitrary componentwise compact map. Proposed change for a future addition: cite Remark 6.18 and Corollary 4.37 if the sharper statement is included. [Efimov, v4, Proposition 2.89, Remark 2.90 and Theorem 2.91](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

### A7. Full faithfulness for finite diagrams (OK)

Ramzi states precisely this in Corollary 4.40 and leaves the proof as an exercise. Chowdhury records it as Remark 6.7 in Mathew’s lectures. Here finite diagrams have the usual finite-limit meaning, indexed by a finite simplicial set. [Ramzi, v1, Corollary 4.40](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21537v1), [Bonn notes, p. 12](https://www.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/de/webfm_send/881/1)

Here is a proof from **prop:Dualizable_Pullbacks**(1). For the empty shape both limits are zero. For a simplex both limits are its value at the initial vertex. Attach a nondegenerate simplex to a smaller simplicial set along its boundary. In each ambient category the new limit is the pullback of the limits on the two pieces over the boundary limit, by gluing cones. First compare the dualizable pullback with the ordinary pullback of those same three dualizable limits using the proposition. Then compare with the ordinary pullback of the three ordinary limits. The second functor is fully faithful by induction, since a limit of fully faithful functors is fully faithful. This proves the claim by finite cell induction.

The finiteness qualification matters. For example, the one-object category $B C_p$ has finitely many arrows and an infinite nerve. Take the constant diagram at $\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb F_p}$. By B7 both the trivial and regular representations lift to compact objects of its dualizable limit. A fully faithful colimit-preserving comparison would be essentially surjective, since its image contains the regular module, a generator of $D(\mathbb F_p[C_p])$. This contradicts the noncompactness of the trivial module there (its periodic free resolution has nonzero Ext into the trivial module in every degree). A future extension of Chapter 7 can cite Corollary 4.40 with the finite-simplicial-set convention.

## B. Proofs

### B1. Semiorthogonal gluing (problem, corrected)

The last sentence reversed the order of the extension. The triangle $i_Dd\to e\to i_Cc$ exhibits $e$ as an extension of $i_Cc$ by $i_Dd$. Corrected the two letters. The gluing functor and its shift are correct.

For the direct check, a boundary $h:i_Cc\to\Sigma i_Dd$ classifies the triangle with middle term $\operatorname{fib}(h)$. Under $\Phi$ its gluing arrow is exactly the adjoint $c\to\Sigma i_C^Ri_Dd$ by the definition of $u$. Conversely, send a triple to the fiber of the adjoint of its gluing arrow. Fiber and boundary recover each other naturally, including the chosen identification of the end terms. Looping this equivalence of spaces of extension classes at the split extension gives the claimed map on mixed mapping spaces. Applying the same argument to suspensions gives the map on mapping spectra. In particular the map is induced by $\Phi$ with the same boundary convention on both sides. [DKSS, published version, Propositions 2.2.11 and 2.3.3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-024-03549-x)

### B2. The mate in exactness of products (OK)

Write $\epsilon_D:j_Dj_D^R\to1$. Since $j_E$ is fully faithful, the mate at $m$ is the composite
\[
Fj_D^Rm \simeq j_E^RGj_Dj_D^Rm
 \xrightarrow{\,j_E^RG\epsilon_D(m)\,} j_E^RGm.
\]
The first identification uses the unit isomorphism for $j_E$ and the specified square. Applying $\psi$ gives precisely $H\epsilon_D$, where $H=\psi j_E^RG$. On the indicated generators, restriction to the small category identifies $H$ with $E_{(x_iF_i)}$, which belongs to the image of $\Phi_D$. Every object in that image inverts $\epsilon_D$ by the triangle identity. Both sides preserve colimits in $\psi$, so the mate is invertible everywhere. The opposite κ-compact localization sequences then give full faithfulness of $G^\vee$ as claimed. For (AB6), the product of the indexing filtered categories is filtered, and functorial Calkin sequences reduce the claim to the compactly generated middle and right terms. No change. [Efimov, v4, Lemma 2.104 and Propositions 2.105–2.106](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

### B3. The essential image of the dual product embedding (OK)

Apply the exact functor $\varphi$ to $jj^R\to1\to p^Rp$. The counit $\varphi jj^R\to\varphi$ is invertible exactly when $\varphi p^Rp=0$, equivalently $\varphi p^R=0$ since $p$ is essentially surjective. Restriction along $\prod p_i$ is $p^\vee$ on exact spectral functors. Its left adjoint is both $(p^R)^\vee$ and $\operatorname{Ind}(\prod p_i^{\mathrm{op}})$, giving the claimed kernel. The one-factor calculation identifies its coordinate generators, and the product formula evaluates them pointwise as asserted.

For self-duality, the epimorphism condition is invertibility of the codiagonal. Ind preserves the pushout in this test and reflects equivalences, and the epimorphisms so obtained in $\mathrm{Pr}^L_{\mathrm{st}}$ are precisely the localizations. Taking opposites is a *covariant* autoequivalence of $\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{perf}}$ and preserves this test. No change. [Efimov, v4, Remark 1.10, Proposition 2.102 and Lemma 2.104](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

### B4. General limits, Step 3 (OK)

With $B=\lim_i D_i$, the composite into $\operatorname{Ind}(\lim_i\operatorname{Ind}(D_i)^\kappa)$ sends each $b\in B$ to the representable on the compatible tuple $(y_i b_i)_i$, by Step 1’s adjunction on the projection cone. Both functors out of $\operatorname{Ind}(B)$ preserve filtered colimits, so this identifies the composite on the whole source. The small functor is fully faithful by the limit formula on mapping animae. Ind preserves full faithfulness, and Step 2 embeds the intermediate target fully faithfully, proving the assertion.

The subsequent zero test concerns a **functor** from a dualizable test category into the dualizable limit. Its compatible cone belongs to the zero component if all projections are zero, since the full subgroupoid of zero functors at each vertex is contractible. This reasoning avoids any assumption of joint conservativity on arbitrary objects of an Ind-category. No change. [Efimov, v4, proof of Theorem 2.91, final two paragraphs](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

### B5. Pushouts of inclusions and of short exact sequences (OK)

### B6. Continuous Calkin mapping spectra (problem, corrected)

The cofiber formula follows by the adjunction to the inclusion of the kernel and exactness of mapping spectra in the second variable. The last sentence invoked **thm:Thomason_Neeman**(2), which requires both the middle category and the kernel to be compactly generated. The kernel here is equivalent to $C$, which is only assumed dualizable. Replaced the invocation by a direct argument. Every object of the quotient is $qX$, and $X$ is a filtered colimit of representables. If $qX$ is compact, its identity factors through one image $q(y(x))$. These images are compact by strong continuity of the quotient. This proves the generation assertion. [Efimov, v4, Proposition 2.62](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

Chapter 3 uses $\mathrm{Mod}_A$ for right modules. Thus $x'$ is right and $\operatorname{hom}_A(x,A)$ is left via multiplication on $A$. The evaluation map $P\otimes_A\operatorname{hom}_A(x,A)\to\operatorname{hom}_A(x,P)$ is an equivalence at $P=A$, hence on all perfect right modules by exactness and retracts. ### B8. (AB6) and the diagonal (OK)

The set example gives exactly the bounded sequences. In the spectral example finite sums distribute over products, so the source on $\pi_0$ consists of families with support in a single finite initial segment. The diagonal family of basis vectors lies outside it. For the positive assertion, the comma category of $(j_s)_s$ under the diagonal has classifying anima $\operatorname{colim}_{j\in J}\prod_s\operatorname{Map}_J(j_s,j)$, by the category-of-elements description. A κ-filtered colimit of animae commutes with a product of fewer than κ factors. Each resulting factor is the classifying anima of $J_{j_s/}$ and hence contractible. The cofinality criterion now applies, including for nondiscrete $J$. No change. [HTT, Theorem 4.1.3.1 and Proposition 5.3.3.3](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~lurie/papers/highertopoi.pdf)

### B10. Countable compact generation via a kernel (OK)

Compactness of $L(d_p)$ lets the image of its identity in $\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(L(d_p),\operatorname{colim}_{p'} L(d_{p'}))=0$ die at a later stage. Closing a countable set under these witnesses and pairwise upper bounds takes countably many steps. For any resulting $Q$ and any compact $c$, a class $c\to L(d_q)$ dies at the chosen witness for $q$. This kills $\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(c,L(x_Q))$ for every compact $c$, including all shifts, so compact generation of $E$ gives $L(x_Q)=0$. No simultaneous choice of coherent nullhomotopies is needed. Countability of the nerve of the poset $Q$ makes $x_Q$ $\omega_1$-compact, and all such countable diagrams of compact objects form an essentially small collection. No change. [Efimov, v4, Proposition 2.11 and Corollary 2.13](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12169v4)

### B11. Operator-ideal models (OK for the archived proposition)

The proposition was withdrawn from Chapter 5 on 25 November. I checked the archived statement and its full proof, including the monoidal assertion. [Archived proof](lecture-model-proof-checks.md#archived-statement-and-proof-former-chapter-5-proposition)

1. For $S_{0+}$ put $p_m=1/m$ and include a fixed positive injective diagonal operator with exponentially decreasing eigenvalues in the family. Choose $\lambda_n>0$ so $\|\lambda_n a_na_n^*\|_{p_m}^{p_m}\le 2^{-n}$ for all $m\le n$, also bounding the operator norm by $2^{-n}$. Completeness and the $p_m$-triangle inequality give convergence for every $m$, with the same operator-norm limit. For the compact ideal, norm closure suffices. The sum $s$ is positive and has zero kernel. Its square root stays in every $S_p$ since its $p$-sum is the $p/2$-sum of $s$. The inequality $a_na_n^*\le\lambda_n^{-1}s$ gives the claimed bounded extension on the dense range of $\sqrt s$ and a common **left** factor. [Douglas, *On majorization, factorization, and range inclusion of operators on Hilbert space*, Theorem 1](https://home.agh.edu.pl/~rudol/Operat/DouglasFactorizationLemma.pdf)
2. Injectivity gives $dA\cong A$ as right modules. Common left factors make their union directed, hence flat. Polar decomposition gives $J^2=J$. The flat resolution $J\to A$ of $A/J$ yields $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(A/J,A/J)=J/J^2=0$ and vanishing in higher degrees. For the telescopes $T_d$, simultaneous factorization of all roots of two operators gives a common containing telescope. Their union is $J$. Tensoring with $J$ is the fiber of the localization unit and generates the kernel from $J$; each proposed telescope maps to zero. This proves both inclusions.
3. Finite projective right modules correspond to summands of finite sums of the fixed Hilbert space. Those sums are again separable, and bounded idempotents split. The transpose converts sides and preserves singular values. On the tensor product of two telescopes the cofinal diagonal has transitions in $J$, by finite-rank approximation or by multiplying the singular values. The one-dimensional tensor unit is a constant telescope with finite-rank identity. The ambient multiplication preserves compact objects since pure tensors of compacts generate the compact objects of its source under finite stable operations and retracts. Thus its right adjoint preserves colimits. Tensoring $i\dashv r$ gives $i\otimes i\dashv r\otimes r$ and identifies the right adjoint to the restricted multiplication with $(r\otimes r)\mu_D^R i$, which preserves colimits. No change to the archive. The separate identification with liquid rigidification is outside this proposition and remains open in the [earlier report](lecture-model-proof-checks.md#the-remaining-liquid-identification).
