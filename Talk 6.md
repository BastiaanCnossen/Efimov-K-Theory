# Semiorthogonal decompositions and finite limits of dualizable categories

## 1. Semi-orthogonal decompositions

**Definition:** Let $C$ in $\PrL_{\st}$ (resp. $\Pr^{\dual}$, $\Cat^{\mathrm{pnt}}_{\infty}$). A "finite semi-orthogonal decomposition" of $C$ in $\PrL_{\st}$ (resp. $\Pr^{\dual}$, $\Cat^{\mathrm{pnt}}_{\infty}$) is a collection of subcategories $C_1, \dots, C_n \subseteq C$ such that:
(1) The inclusions $C_i \hookrightarrow C$ are strongly continuous
(2) For each $i > j$, we have $\Hom_C(X,Y)=0$ for $X \in C_i$, $Y \in C_j$.
(c) The objects of $\bigcup_i C_i$ generate $C$ as a stable category.
In this case, we write$$C = \lra{C_1, \dots, C_n}.$$
**Lemma:** Given strongly continuous functors $i_1\colon C \to E$ and $i_2\colon D \to E$, these two functors exhibit $E$ as a semi-orthogonal decomposition of $C$ and $D$* if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:
(1) $i_1$ admits a left adjoint $i_1^L$, and $i_2$ admits a right adjoint $i_2^R$.
(2) The maps $i_1^Li_1 \to \id_C$ and $\id_D \to i_2^Ri_2$ are isomorphisms.
(3) We have $i_1^Li_2=0$ (equivalently, $i_2^Ri_1 =0$).
(4) The canonical zero sequence$$i_2i_2^R \to \id_E \to i_1i_1^L$$(obtained from the fact that $i_2^Ri_1=0$) is a cofiber sequence.
**Proof:** First assume that $i_1$ and $i_2$ are fully faithful, and we have $E = \lra{C,D}$. Condition (2) holds as $i_1$ and $i_2$ are fully faithful. We have $0 = \Hom_C(i_2d,i_1c) = \Hom_C(i_1^Li_2d,c)$ for all $c$ and $d$, showing that $i^L_1i_2d = 0$, giving (3). Define$$\phi(e) := \cofib(i_2i_2^Re \to e).$$We claim that $\phi(E) \subseteq i_1C$. Indeed, for $c \in C$ we have$$\phi(i_1c) = \cofib(i_2i_2^Ri_1c \to i_1c) = \cofib(0 \to i_1c) = i_1c$$ and for $d \in D$ we have$$\phi(i_2d) = \cofib(i_2i_2^Ri_2d \to i_2d) = \cofib(i_2d \to i_2d) = 0.$$We then obtain a functor $i_1^L\colon E \to C$ satisfying $i_1i_1^L = \phi$. We claim that $i_1^L$ is a left adjoint to $i_1$. Indeed, for all $e \in E$ we have a fiber sequence $$\Hom(i_1^Le,c) \to \Hom_E(e,i_1c) \to \Hom_E(i_2i_2^Re,i_1c) =0,$$showing that the first map is an isomorphism. This gives (1). Finally, (4) follows from the definition of $\phi$.

Now assume that conditions (1)-(4) are satisfied. By assumption (2), the two functors are fully faithful. For each $c,d$, we have $0 = \Hom_C(i_1^Li_2d,c) = \Hom_E(i_2d,i_1c)$, showing the orthogonality condition. For $e \in E$ we have a cofiber sequence $$i_2i_2^R e \to e \to i_1i_1^Le.$$ Since both $i_2i_2^R e $ and $i_1i_1^Le$ are in the essential images of $i_1$ and $i_2$, every $e$ is in the stable subcategory generated by these images. This finishes the proof.

## The gluing construction

**Definition:** Let $F\colon D \to C$ be an exact functor between stable categories. We denote by$$C \oplus_F D := \{(x,y,\phi) \mid x \in C, y \in D, \phi\colon x \to F(y)\} = D \times_C \Ar(C).$$There are canonical inclusions$$i_1\colon C \to C \oplus_F D, \qquad c \mapsto (c[-1],0,0)$$and$$i_2\colon D \to C \oplus_F D, \qquad d \mapsto (0,d,0).$$Note that $i_2$ is always strongly continuous.

**Proposition:** Let $F\colon D \to C$ be an accessible exact functor between dualizable categories. Let $E = C \oplus_F D$. Then the following two conditions are equivalent:
(1) $F$ is continuous
(2) $E$ is dualizable and $i_1$ and $i_2$ are strongly continuous.
**Proof:** We have $\Hom(i_1c,(x,y,\phi)) = \Hom((c[-1],0,0),(x,y,\phi)) = \fib(\Hom(c[-1],x) \xrightarrow{\phi_*} \Hom(c[-1],F(y))) = \Hom(c[-1],\fib(x \to F(y))= \Hom(c,\cofib(x \to F(y)),$so we see that $i_1$ admits a right adjoint given by $i_1^R(x,y,\phi) = \cofib(\phi).$ So we see that if $F$ is continuous, so is $i_1^R$, so $i_1$ is strongly continuous.

Conversely, if $i_1$ is strongly continuous, then $i_1^Ri_2\colon D \to C$ is continuous. Moreover, it is identified with $F$, since $i_1^Ri_2(d) = \cofiber(0 \to Fd) = Fd$.

**Proposition:** Let $E \in \PrL_{\st}$ and assume that $E = \lra{C,D}$.  Let $\pi_1\colon E \to C$ be the left adjoint of $i_1$ and let $\pi_2$ be the right adjoint of $i_2$. Consider $T \in \PrL_{\st}$.
(1) A functor $F\colon T \to E$ is strongly continuous if and only if $\pi_1F$ and $\pi_2F$ are strongly continuous. Moreover, there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition$$\Fun^{LL}(T,E) = \lra{Fun^{LL}(T,C), \Fun^{LL}(T,D)}.$$(2) A functor $G\colon E \to T'$ is strongly continuous if and only if both $Gi_1$ and $Gi_2$ are strongly continuous, and$$\Fun^{LL}(E,T') = \lra{\Fun^{LL}(D,T'), \Fun^{LL}(C,T')}.$$**Proof:** Consider the two inclusions$$j_1\colon \Fun^{LL}(T,C) \hookrightarrow \Fun^{LL}(T,E), \qquad f \mapsto i_1 \circ f$$ and $j_2\colon \Fun^{LL}(T,D) \hookrightarrow \Fun^{LL}(T,E), \qquad g \mapsto i_2 \circ g.$$ We have adjoints $$j_1^L(f) := \pi_1f, \qquadtext{ and } j_2^R(g) := \pi_2g.$$The fact hat $i_1$ and $i_2$ are fully faithful means that $j_1^Lj_1f = \pi_1i_1f = f$ and similarly $j_2^Rj_2g = g$, so also $j_1$ and $j_2$ are fully faithful. Since $\pi_1i_2=0$, we get $j_1^Lj_2(f) = 0$. So this gives the desired orthogonal decomposition.

## 3. Finite limits in $\Pr^{\dual}$

**Proposition:** Let $C$ be a dualizable category. For every $x \in C^{\omega_1}$, there exists a functor $\phi\colon (\Q,\leq) \to C$ such that:
(1) We have $x \simeq \colim_{a \in \Q} \phi(a)$
(2) For all $a \in \Q$, we have $\colim_{b < a} \phi(b) \iso \phi(a)$.
(3) For all $a < b$, the map $\phi(a) \to \phi(b)$ is copmact.
**Proof:** Consider the subposet $\Z[\frac{1}{2}] \subseteq \Q$. Pick a sequence of compact morphisms $x_1 \to x_2 \to \dots$ such that $x \simeq \colim_i x_i$. We extend this to $\Z \to C$ by setting $x_{-n} = 0$. Since every compact morphism is a composite of two compact morphisms, we may extend this inductively to functors $\phi'_n\colon \frac{1}{2^n}\Z \to C$ such that $\phi'_{n+1}\vert_{\frac{1}{2^n}\Z} \cong \phi'_n$ such that all transition maps are compact. This gives rise to a functor $\phi'\colon \Z[\frac{1}{2}] \to C$. We then define$$\phi(a) := \colim_{b < a} \phi'(b).$$
**Lemma:** Let $C$ be a dualizable category, and let $D \subseteq C$ be a full stable subcategory closed under colimits. Then there exists a largest dualizable subcategory $E \subseteq D$ such that $E \hookrightarrow C$ is strongly continuous.
**Proof:** We let $E$ be the full subcategory generated under colimits by the objects $\colim(F\colon \Q \to D)$ where $F$ satisfies that $F(a) \to F(b)$ is compact in $C$ for each $a < b$. By replacing $F$ with $F'(a) = \colim_{b < a} F(b)$, we may assume that $F$ satisfies the property that $\colim_{b < a} F(b) \iso F(a)$.

For all $a$, we have $F(a) \in E$, because$$F(a) = \colim(F\colon \Q_{\leq a} \to D).$$We now show that $E$ is dualizable. We need to show that $\colim\colon \Ind(E) \to E$ admits a left adjoint $\hat{y}\colon E \to \Ind(E)$. We may check this on objects. For an object of $e$, we write it as $\colim F$. One can then show that the formal colimit $F(0) \to F(1) \to F(2) \to \dots$ satisfies the universal property of $\hat{y}(\colim F)$.

From this, it also follows that the inclusion $E \to C$ is strongly continuous. (Why?)

Finally, assume that $E' \subseteq D$ is a stable subcategory such that the inclusion into $C$ is strongly continuous. We need to show that $E' \subseteq E$. Pick $x \in (E')^{\omega_1}$. As before, we may find a functor $F\colon (\Q,\leq) \to E'$ with $x = \colim_{\Q}F$ such that each $F(a) \to F(b)$ is compact. Then it follows that $x \in E$, and thus $E' \subseteq E$.

**Definition:** Let $F\colon C \to D$ be a strongly continuous functor between dualizable category. Let $\ker(F)$ denote the kernel in $\PrL_{\st}$. We define the *dualizable kernel of $F$*, denoted $\ker^{\dual}(F) \subseteq C$, as the largest dualizable subcategory $E \subseteq \ker(F)$ such that the inclusion $E \hookrightarrow C$ is strongly continuous. This exists by the previous lemma.

**Proposition:** The sequence $\ker^{\dual}(F) \to C \xrightarrow{F} D$ is a fiber sequence in $\Pr^{\dual}$.
**Proof:** Consider a functor $G\colon T \to C$ in $\Pr^{\dual}$ such that $F \circ G = 0$. Then $\Im(G) \subseteq \ker(F)$. By the results recalled earlier (in the notes this would be replaced by a reference), the image is dualizable, and $\Im(G) \hookrightarrow C$ is strongly continuous. It thus follows that $\Im(G) \subseteq \ker^{\dual}(F)$. Since $\ker^{\dual}(F) \hookrightarrow C$ is a monomorphism, this proves the claim.
